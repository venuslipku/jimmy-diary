# Daniel's Essay

**Collection:** Successful Harvard Law School Essays (2025)  
**Source:** <https://business.thecrimson.com/successful-harvard-law-school-essays-2025>  
**Essay #:** 2

## Prompt

This essay responds to the following prompt: The Admissions Committee makes every effort to understand who you are as an individual and potential Harvard Law School student and graduate. Please share how your experiences, background, and/or interests have shaped you and will shape your engagement in the HLS community and the legal profession.

## Essay

In the spring of my junior year, the Union-Philanthropic Literary Society (UPLS), a 234-year-old organization at Hampden-Sydney College, elected me its president. The position came with several prescribed roles: preside over weekly debates in a black robe on a nineteenth-century throne, appoint members as officers, and plan the Society’s numerous social events. Informally, however, it also required that I ensure that the tone of the Society’s proceedings accord with its values of “truth, virtue, and friendship,” and it has been this part of the work that has proven the most challenging, rewarding, and instructive for how I will pursue my career in law.

When I joined UPLS, its leaders impressed upon me the importance of these values and exemplified most of them. They modeled friendship by getting to know all the members, and they were always available to work on my speeches for the next debate. The President in particular helped me grow intellectually by subjecting my beliefs to the level of scrutiny that truth ought to withstand. At the same time, however, I saw instances of alcohol-fueled behavior within the Society that resulted in hard words and broken relationships. The Society, like the rest of campus, revolved around binge drinking. Though the leadership had inherited rather than created this culture, and though they made progress in changing it, old organizations turn slowly. When they graduated and I was elected President, I understood that it was now up to me to shape the Society’s culture and continue its tradition of academic and personal mentorship.

One way I do so involves our debates. UPLS meets every Sunday to debate a topic (often a hot-button issue), and members are cumulatively responsible for presenting at least six speeches on it. To prepare members for these speeches, I work with them on ideas, listen to and critique practice speeches, and help them formulate what they think to be true rather than what will win the argument. We go over charitable interpretation, what to do with one’s hands while speaking, how to vary tone and pacing to avoid sounding monotonous, and many other forensic techniques. I work to direct the entire enterprise toward the truth, which UPLS has shown me can be approached by presenting the strongest possible argument for a variety of positions in the context of reasoned debate.

I spend more of my time, however, in academic and personal mentorship. I frequently review papers and talk through ideas for assignments with other members, either during my tutoring hours or while shooting pool in the student center. I helped my roommate, a sophomore involved in the Society, overcome his habit of procrastinating by planning out his weeks to get work done earlier with time to review it with others. Under my mentorship, another member who came into the Society with beliefs based on a few disturbing internet subcultures has since begun to read broadly and integrate his beliefs into the broader culture. I have encouraged the other leaders of the Society to make themselves similarly available, and I see the positive influence we have had as friends on the other members.

Finally, I have been working to address the Society’s outstanding problems with alcohol. I wanted the Society to look different from the rest of campus, to be an environment where people could be comfortable not drinking at all or drinking in moderation. Since my election, I have organized a variety of non-alcohol-related events ranging from croquet tournaments on the front lawn to late-night poker games. When the Society does serve drinks, I step in when someone begins to binge. The goal is to make the culture one of moderate consumption where abstinence is acceptable and thereby to influence the conduct of the Society’s members for the better. In my tenure, I have had considerable success.

As my graduation approaches, I hope to continue my education in a field that revolves around advocacy, honest reformulation, and collaborative exertion—law. Though I do not know in what area of law I want to work, only that I am attracted to litigation, I know that UPLS has shaped my choices of law school and made me value excellence. I intend to be part of HLS’s intellectual community that teaches its students to be not just on the winning team but on the right side of issues. I want to join a community like HLS that comes closer to the truth by engaging with different perspectives respectfully, and I intend to be a voice that values other views and encourages similar behavior in others. I also hope to be the person who others can come to for a citation, counterargument, or cheering up. In short, I want to bring a little bit of UPLS to HLS, where I will continue to pursue truth, virtue, and friendship on my path to a career in law.

## Professional Review by Austin LSAT Prep

Daniel has not selected a specific area of law to pursue, and he knows only that he is attracted to litigation. He cannot tell Harvard Law School what he hopes to achieve as a lawyer, so his challenge is to present a persuasive case for why he, Daniel, will be a worthy addition to HLS and the legal profession.

The strategy that Daniel successfully employs for his Personal Statement is to describe a life experience applicable in the field of law as compelling evidence that he will be an excellent lawyer.

Wisely, Daniel does not simply remind HLS that he was elected president of a venerable student organization, impressive as that may be. Instead, he honestly describes a difficult situation that he addressed: society members were engaging in harmful drinking behaviors, which he believes undermines the ideals ("truth, virtue, and friendship") that made the institution special.

Sometimes illegal and/or unethical behaviors or practices permeate an organization so completely that they are accepted as unchangeable. As an officer of the law, a lawyer must be willing to take action and provide ethical guidance. Daniel not only demonstrates personal integrity but also his ability to lead others to uproot the deeply embedded binge drinking culture.

Daniel also describes his experience in debate, a desirable skill for an attorney, and his mentorship. However, his clear-eyed recognition of a situation harmful to his organization and his willingness to take decisive action as a leader is what makes him stand out to HLS.
