## Prompt

Leadership-Focused Essay Prompt: "What experiences have shaped who you are, how you invest in others, and what kind of leader you want to become?"

## Essay

“Counsel with your feet, not your voice.” This phrase, ingrained in me during my summers as a YMCA camp counselor, has profoundly influenced my leadership vision over the past decade. Though supervising five-year-olds differs vastly from leading pharmaceutical manufacturing campaigns, the principle of leading by example remains central to my approach.

Truly inspirational leadership combines empathy, approachability, and proactivity. To better understand and support patients with uncured medical conditions, I actively engage with service organizations such as Kesem, Misericordia, and Camp One Step. These experiences have deepened my empathy and reinforced my commitment to impactful leadership in healthcare.

Starting my career at a company with over 80,000 employees, I discovered the importance of leading through action rather than rhetoric. To ensure the effectiveness and safety of every product, impactful leaders must be both contributors and project managers. As a manufacturing program manager, I embraced this philosophy by conducting on-site visits with vendors and shadowing technicians. Through this hands-on approach, I effectively guided my team through risk management and process improvements.

While leading pilot-scale engineering batches from conception to analysis, careful planning is crucial, but day-to-day unexpected challenges are unavoidable. By implementing trending and tracking technologies, I monitored for unexplained excursions well before issues became mission-critical, allowing for swift interventions and minimizing product loss or reactor downtime through proactive leadership.

By pursuing an MBA at Harvard, I aim to enhance my leadership capabilities further, continuing to “counsel with my feet” and inspire others through direct, impactful action.

## Professional Review by Applicant Lab

This applicant has clearly had an impactful career so far; one that’s allowed them to cultivate and demonstrate promising leadership potential! The essay starts strong: the enduring YMCA camp counselor lesson paints a vivid picture, while still respecting HBS’s desire for brevity. The essay is also strong in highlighting the breadth of the applicant’s experience: the reference to working with vendors and technicians emphasizes interpersonal skills across multi-faceted teams, and introducing new tracking technologies demonstrates initiative.

The essay could have been optimized even further by trimming away superfluous information. Broad-sweeping pronouncements about which traits “truly inspirational”, “impactful leaders” have were a wasted opportunity: that word-count would have been better allocated to describing very specific details around the candidate’s specific actions taken during specific anecdotes. Another place to optimize could have been to remove information I’d already know from elsewhere in the application form/ resume (e.g. volunteer activities; company’s number of employees), as well as the unnecessary closing line about the desire to go to HBS (which is already assumed!).

In ApplicantLab, my HBS module is one of several that provides multiple step-by-step lessons to teach you how to optimize your essay; this one was strong, but could have been even stronger by trimming some vaguer parts and replacing them with vivid, memorable details unique to the candidate.
