# Kevin's Essay

**Collection:** Successful Harvard Law School Essays (2025)  
**Source:** <https://business.thecrimson.com/successful-harvard-law-school-essays-2025>  
**Essay #:** 4

## Prompt

This essay responds to the following prompt: What motivates you to pursue law? How does attending law school align with your ambitions, goals, and vision for your future?

## Essay

I sit in the center of a pine table, blissfully unaware. Before me is a baseball, a gold coin, a pencil, and a computer mouse. “Choose one,” my dad beckons. Stumbling over the hem of my hanbok, I make my way forward. After a pause, I grab the mouse, and whispers fill the room: “He’ll be just like his dad!”

Every so often, my parents fondly recall my doljabi, a Korean tradition where one-year-old babies choose their “fate” among objects symbolizing different careers on their first birthday (often reflections of their parents’ wishful dreams for them). The computer mouse was my dad’s own touch, a sci-fi-loving tech nerd, whose Lego-building skills and Star Wars fanaticism I adopted even by age four. Following in his footsteps, I entered college undeterred in my goal to be a software engineer just like him.

My first semester brimmed with naive excitement, as I eagerly completed every bonus project in my computer science class and even taught myself advanced programming concepts. However, as the late-night talks with my hallmates about our aspirations faded into a mere memory, I found myself habitually watching the news, searching for hope amid the uncertainty brought by the pandemic. Instead, I saw a country wrought with health inequities, politicized misinformation, and violent acts of discrimination, and an overwhelming urgency to redress them stirred within me. Though I donated to important causes and educated myself, I felt increasingly powerless with each passing day, distant from suffering communities as I was sheltered from their pain by my TV screen.

On March 16, 2021, just a few miles from my campus home, a horrific shooting of Atlanta spas killed eight innocent bystanders, most of whom looked like me. Watching the evening news, I wrestled with a sobering mix of hopelessness and guilt: What could have prevented this? It could have just as easily been me. Upon sharing my reflections, I was met with my dad’s oft-quoted Korean proverb: “Jin Insa Dae Cheonmyeong” or “Fate ultimately decides, but man does what he can.” The once encouraging words no longer felt acceptable though; I couldn’t bring myself to leave this tragedy up to “fate.”

The next day, while learning about sorting algorithms in my computer science class, I began to see a disconnect between my academic spaces and reality, and wondered how my education could help me respond to issues of equity and justice. When the Stop Asian Hate movement gained traction, I received a stern warning from my family to not attend the rallies and avoid causing controversy. Rather than passively enduring injustices, I wanted to redirect the generational Korean perseverance I inherited into proactively addressing them. The following Sunday, my pastor offered thoughts and prayers, but refrained from further action in order to remain “apolitical.” Although I felt disheartened by his neutral stance, I was also motivated to find ways to apply the principles of my faith, helping others in practice.

As these environments formed core aspects of my identity, I held doubts about my capacity to create change, unable to shake the sense that the voices around me rang against my own. However, I noticed that whenever hardship arose, lawyers were the first to respond. Curious as to whether I too could have a tangible impact in a legal career, I interned with my local district attorney’s office. On my first day, I was assigned to research the militancy of “sovereign citizen” movements. While the task initially seemed trivial, I was later told that my analysis convinced the presiding judge that an armed sovereign citizen group marching across the state was a significant flight risk. Though I played a small role alongside the attorneys to protect our community, my familiar feeling of powerlessness had disappeared.

My search for avenues to channel my intellectual curiosity, cultural values, and faith toward serving others has guided my journey in the law. At the start of my third year, I grafted a new major into my academic focus that finally allowed me to explore fundamental normative questions, deepening my philosophical understanding of the law and its capacity to address our technologically driven future. Interning for a legal nonprofit, I attended criminal court daily and saw firsthand the responsibility of lawyers to zealously advocate for their clients and community, never feigning passive indifference to clear suffering. Now, as a legal assistant for a state court, I serve individuals who turn to the law as their last hope: some barred from employment due to their criminal pasts, others burdened by a lack of financial support for their children. Empathizing with their stories through our conversations, I have realized that the law is also a powerful instrument of the grace integral to my faith, giving a second chance to those who desperately need it. In these ways, my legal career thus far has enabled me to act beyond the perceived limits of my identity, equipping me to address the suffering I see. However, the most meaningful thing I have gained is hope, a hope grounded in the idea that my choices, rather than my fate, are the driving force in creating a more just world, even if I didn’t pick up a gavel on my first birthday.

## Professional Review by The Harvard Crimson

Harvard Law School’s Statement of Purpose is a two page opportunity to highlight your potential — beyond what test scores and resume lines say. Kevin accomplishes this with exemplary storytelling. Throughout his essay, not only does Kevin depict his character, but he also demonstrates his efficacy.

Kevin’s story follows a clear trajectory. After a compelling hook, he smoothly introduces himself, sprinkling important details about his background which add depth to his profile. Then we reach the peak of the story: the internal conflict Kevin experiences caused by the clash of his traditional lifestyle and values with his empathy and responsiveness to the state of the world. After this climax, Kevin brings the story to its conclusion as he resolves the conflict. The key to his emotional resolution? His empowerment and the impact he has through legal work. Kevin neatly completes his story by cleverly connecting his opening hook back to his concluding sentence. The contrast between the beginning sentence and ending conclusion demonstrate his personal growth and reflection.

Honing further into the success of Kevin’s essay: how he writes about his internal conflict demonstrates his connection to family, academic prowess, and determined disposition. In the third paragraph we learn how Kevin succeeds in his original intellectual pursuit. We later discover Kevin’s nature for empathy as he narrates his internal conflict when viewing the political climate. Instead of acknowledging this and moving on, Kevin changes. He acts to resolve his conflict, which highlights his can-and-will-do attitude. Kevin illustrates specific moments and accomplishments from his experiences exploring the legal field, backing up his passion for law with substantial evidence of excellence.

Overall, Kevin’s narrative is so compelling because he describes a reciprocal relationship, where both the legal field and the person positively impact each other. Kevin creates a beautiful and complete narrative in this story. He clearly explains why he is passionate about law while delivering evidence of his actionable character and academic prowess. And he manages to appeal to a widespread struggle we all share while also making it sound intricately unique: the dilemma between balancing your past with thinking of your future.
